    About the temperature model 
    def predict_lst(year, day):
    t = 2 * np.pi * day / 365
    return (302.008 - 0.0549 * (year - 2000)
            - 0.651 * np.sin(t)   + 9.866 * np.cos(t)
            - 0.395 * np.sin(2*t) - 1.668 * np.cos(2*t))
    • Harmonic regression on MODIS Terra MOD11A1 daytime land-surface temperature (1 km). Output is in Kelvin and shown here in °C.
    • day is day of year (1–365). The two sine/cosine pairs capture the annual and semi-annual cycle; the year term is the long-run trend.
    • The sowing start is the sowing window (6 weeks for wheat and barley, 2 weeks for corn) whose daily predicted LST stays closest to the chosen target (lowest RMSE). Defaults: wheat 20 °C, barley 17.5 °C, corn 21 °C.
    • The sowing start is given as a range. The same search is run on the two 20% curves: a warmer-than-usual year reaches the target on one day, a cooler one on another, and any start between them is reasonable. The aim date is the centre curve's best fit.
    • The curve is cyclical, so a target is often crossed twice a year (cooling and warming). A second sowing date is offered when both dates average within 1 °C of the target and the temperature moves at least 1.5 °C away from it between them.
    • The shaded band shows the spread of real readings around the curve: two more harmonic fits with the same terms, one with 20% of readings above it and one with 20% below, so the middle 60% of readings fall inside the band. Faint dotted lines show two more fits for the minimum and maximum readings.
    • The model has no location input, so Perth, Brisbane and Melbourne currently share the same curve.
    • Rainfall is a separate curve for each location: a 3-harmonic fit to NASA POWER daily precipitation (MERRA-2, bias-corrected, 2001–2025). With Consider rainfall on, a sowing window only qualifies if its typical rain over the 2 weeks before and during sowing is in the crop's range (wheat and barley 5–25, corn 10–35 mm/week). Temperature then picks the best of the qualifying windows.
